About the workshop

Why a heterogeneous interaction perspective?

Human-AI interaction is inherently heterogeneous: it involves learners, AI agents, tasks and artefacts, and the exchanges among them differ in kind, from posing a question to an AI, to acting on its response, to revising a shared artefact.

Heterogeneous Interaction Network Analysis (HINA) represents different types of entities and their relations within a single network. This can then model who interacts with whom and what the interaction concerns together.

Building on the first HINA workshop at LAK26, which introduced the method, this second edition focuses on applying HINA to human-AI interaction. In response to participants' feedback, it devotes more time to hands-on practice and discussion. The workshop combines a hands-on tutorial, contributed talks selected through an open call, and an extended interactive session in which participants analyze provided datasets or their own.

The method

HINA: a three-level analytical framework

Individual level

Process metrics

Quantifies the quantity and diversity of each learner's heterogeneous interactions, for example how engagement is distributed between AI and peers.

Dyadic level

Representative associations

Explores observed edge weights against null models to identify interactions in the learning process that occur more often than expected by chance.

Meso level

Interaction profiles

Uses a nonparametric clustering method to find clusters of learners with similar interaction profiles, with the number of clusters inferred from the data.

Topics of interest

We welcome work applying HINA to analyze learning process data, including:

  • Applications of HINA to human-AI interaction data in individual or collaborative learning
  • Applications of HINA to other learning process data, such as collaborative, multimodal or dialogue data
  • Methodological reflections on HINA and its connections with other learning analytics methods

Participate

All are welcome

The workshop is open to everyone: researchers, graduate students and practitioners in learning analytics and related fields who are interested in human-AI interaction, network analysis, or learning process data. It is particularly relevant if you collect data from AI-supported learning environments, such as student–chatbot dialogues or logs of AI-assisted collaborative work, and want to go beyond descriptive summaries.

No prior experience with network analysis or programming is required.

Notes for participants

  • Your own laptop with a stable internet connection
  • Optionally, a dataset in which learners' interactions with AI (and, where relevant, with peers or artefacts) can be identified
  • Sample datasets will be provided if you prefer a prepared example

You will leave with

  • Practical skills to build heterogeneous interaction networks from logs or dialogues and run individual, dyadic and meso-level analyses
  • Reusable scripts, sample datasets and tutorial materials
  • Connections with a community working on network-based analysis of human-AI interaction
